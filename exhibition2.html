Youth Voices Rise Amid Doubts and Criticism

EXHIBITION 02

There is a contradiction in telling young people that they are the hope of the nation while questioning their place whenever they speak about its problems. They are encouraged to become informed citizens, to care about their communities, and to take responsibility for the country's future. Yet, when they begin questioning political decisions, criticizing social conditions, or joining movements that demand change, their participation can suddenly become unwelcome.

This contradiction becomes especially visible in the experiences of young Filipinos who take part in demonstrations and political movements. Events such as the Lakbayan ng Timog Katagalugan reflect how people come together to bring attention to concerns affecting their communities. Among those who participate are young people who refuse to remain silent about the realities surrounding them. Their involvement challenges the idea that political awareness and resistance should only belong to adults or those already holding positions of influence.

Instead of engaging with the substance of their concerns, young people can be dismissed as inexperienced, easily influenced, or incapable of understanding complicated issues. Their age becomes the focus rather than the issues they are raising. This raises an important question: if young people are expected to inherit the consequences of decisions being made today, why should they not have a say in those decisions?

Art has long been part of how people respond to injustice and express political beliefs. A photograph can preserve a moment of collective resistance. An illustration can challenge those in power. A protest sign or a social media post can turn an individual concern into a message shared by many. These expressions remind us that young people are not simply repeating what others tell them. They are observing, questioning, interpreting, and forming positions of their own.

Being young does not make a person's concerns irrelevant. Disagreement should not be mistaken for disrespect, and questioning authority should not automatically be treated as a lack of understanding. If the youth are expected to care about the nation, they must also be allowed to speak about what they believe needs to change.
